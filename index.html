02 — Philosophy

Build things that make life simpler. Lead in a way that makes teams stronger. Never stop learning.

Success is not only a result. It is the habits you repeat, the people you help and the honesty with which you work. I want to build a company and a life I’m proud of when nobody is watching.

01

People before features

A product is only good if it solves a real problem for a real person. Before adding anything to Twimee, I ask who needs it and what they will stop struggling with because of it.

02

Simplicity is respect

Making something simple takes more effort than making it complicated. A clear design, a short sentence and a fast page show respect for the user’s time.

03

Trust is the real product

In any marketplace, people trade only when they trust each other. Honesty, clear rules and fair treatment matter more than any feature or advertisement.

04

Learn fast, fail early

Mistakes are cheaper when they are small and early. I test ideas, listen to feedback, fix what is wrong and keep moving. A failed experiment is information, not defeat.

05

Lead by serving

Volleyball taught me that a captain’s job is to prepare the team, not to take every shot. I give clear direction, share credit and take responsibility when things go wrong.

06

Discipline over motivation

Motivation comes and goes; routines stay. Planning my week, protecting study time and reviewing progress keep me moving on the days I don’t feel like it.

07

Community over audience

An audience watches; a community participates. I want Twimee to be a place where people help each other, share and grow together.

08

Long-term over loud

Quick attention fades quickly. I prefer steady progress, good relationships and a reputation that grows year after year.

09

Humble and curious

There is always someone who knows more. I ask questions, read widely, respect teachers and mentors, and treat every person I meet as someone I can learn from.

03 — Twimee.com

Everything
in one app.

Twimee is a social marketplace that combines the social side of the internet with the practical side of trade.

The problem. People chat in one app, browse in another, buy in a third and sell in a fourth. Trust is scattered and time is wasted switching. Small sellers struggle to be seen, and buyers struggle to know whom to trust.

The vision. One simple place to build a profile, connect with others, discover what’s around you and buy or sell with confidence, giving everyday people and small businesses the same opportunities as large ones.

Connect 01

Build a profile, follow people and businesses, and join conversations around shared interests.

Discover 02

Find products, services and creators through the people and communities you already trust.

Trade 03

Buy and sell where you connect, with a simple and transparent experience.

07 — Journal

Thoughts on building, learning and leading.

Startups

Why I’m building Twimee

Think about how you use your phone to trade or discover something. You chat in one app, search in another, compare in a third and pay somewhere else. Every switch costs time and trust.

Twimee started from that frustration. I believe the social and commercial sides of life belong together, because people already buy from people they know and trust. A social marketplace turns that habit into a simple product.

My rule is to keep it small and clear first: a good profile, a good feed and a safe way to trade. Everything else grows from there, step by step, with the community’s feedback.

Student life

Building a company while studying

Studying and building a company at the same time sounds exciting, but it demands honest time management. I plan each week in advance: classes and exams first, then club work, then Twimee, with space for sport and rest.

Business classes give me frameworks for marketing, finance and strategy, and Twimee gives me a place to test them. When a lecture explains customer segmentation, I can apply it to my own product that same week.

The hardest lesson has been to say no. Not every opportunity is the right one, and focus is what lets a student founder keep going.

Leadership

What volleyball taught me about leading

In volleyball every point depends on a chain: receive, set, attack. If one link fails, the team loses the point. That is how I now see every project.

As captain, I learned to stay calm after a bad rally, to encourage rather than blame, and to trust teammates with their roles. I use the same habits when I lead event teams and the Twimee team.

A good captain doesn’t need to score every point. A good captain makes sure everyone is ready to.

Learning

How I learn new tools quickly

I learn by making something real. When I picked up Power BI, I didn’t only watch tutorials; I built a small dashboard from data I cared about. The same goes for Excel, Canva and video editors.

I also use AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and DeepSeek as study partners: to explain a concept simply, suggest ideas and review drafts. But I always check the facts and write the final work myself.

A short weekly review keeps the habit alive: what did I learn, what slowed me down, and what is my next priority?

08 — Resources

Tools, habits and reading I recommend.

Data

Excel & Power BI

Learn pivot tables, lookup formulas and simple dashboards. Turning raw data into a clear chart is one of the most useful business skills.

Design

Canva

A fast way to make posters, social posts, presentations and logos without design training.

Video

CapCut & InShot

Easy mobile editors for short videos, product promos and event highlights.

AI

ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek

Good for research, brainstorming and first drafts. Use them as assistants, and verify what they tell you.

Office

Word & PowerPoint

Still the quickest way to write clear reports and present ideas to a team.

Marketing

Google Analytics

Shows where visitors come from and what they do, so you improve with evidence rather than guesses.

Habit

Weekly review

Every Sunday, write what went well, what didn’t and your top three priorities for the next week.

Habit

One-page plans

Before a project, write its goal, audience, steps and deadline on one page. Clarity saves days.

Reading

Business books

The Lean Startup, Zero to One, Atomic Habits and Good to Great are well-known starting points on startups, habits and leadership.
